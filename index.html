About

Connecting device physics with manufacturable solar technology

I am an EPSRC-funded PhD researcher in Physics at the University of Sheffield, specialising in photovoltaic materials and device physics. My work centres on scalable spray-coated perovskite solar cells and their integration onto carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer substrates.

My experience spans cleanroom and glovebox fabrication, solution processing, vacuum deposition, laser patterning, electrical and materials characterisation, and drift-diffusion modelling. I am particularly interested in translating high-performance laboratory devices into reliable, lightweight, and scalable photovoltaic technologies.

Research

Current research themes

01

Scalable spray coating

Developing and optimising spray-coated perovskite absorbers and functional layers, with emphasis on drying, morphology, uniformity, and process reproducibility.

02

Lightweight structural PV

Integrating thin-film perovskite devices onto conductive CFRP substrates for lightweight, high-specific-power, and potentially multifunctional applications.

03

Interfaces and morphology

Investigating substrate roughness, interfacial engineering, and processing conditions to improve film formation, crystallisation, and photovoltaic performance.

04

Mini-module development

Scaling laboratory-scale cells into series-connected mini-modules through laser patterning, area definition, interconnection, and scalable deposition strategies.

05

Advanced characterisation

Linking device performance to optical, structural, electrical, and morphological properties using J–V, EQE, spectroscopy, microscopy, diffraction, and profilometry.

06

Device modelling

Using SCAPS-1D and related drift-diffusion tools to study wide-bandgap perovskites, interfaces, recombination, parameter sensitivity, and multijunction potential.
