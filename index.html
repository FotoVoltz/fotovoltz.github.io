About

From device physics to scalable fabrication

I am an EPSRC-funded PhD researcher in Physics at the University of Sheffield. My work focuses on scalable perovskite photovoltaics, particularly spray-coated devices and their integration with lightweight carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer substrates.

My background combines photovoltaic device modelling, thin-film fabrication, materials characterisation, and solar-cell performance analysis. I am interested in translating laboratory-scale advances into reliable, manufacturable solar technologies for emerging applications.

Research

Research themes

01

Scalable perovskite fabrication

Developing spray-coating processes for charge-selective layers and perovskite absorbers, with attention to uniformity, drying, and process reproducibility.

02

Lightweight structural PV

Integrating perovskite photovoltaic devices with ITO-coated CFRP substrates for lightweight and potentially multifunctional solar applications.

03

Device physics and interfaces

Investigating interface engineering, passivation, transport layers, performance losses, hysteresis, and stability in perovskite solar cells.

04

Minimodules

Working toward interconnected small-area modules through laser patterning, device-area definition, and scalable deposition strategies.

05

Characterisation

Using electrical, optical, structural, and morphological techniques to connect material quality with device-level performance.

06

PV modelling

Applying drift-diffusion modelling to understand photovoltaic devices, wide-bandgap perovskites, parameter sensitivity, and multijunction potential.
