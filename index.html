About

From photovoltaic device physics to scalable fabrication

My research sits at the intersection of thin-film photovoltaic fabrication, materials science, device physics, and scalable manufacturing. I work with perovskite solar cells from early-stage process development through device fabrication, measurement, analysis, and mini-module scale-up.

My experimental background includes spray coating, spin coating, thermal and electron-beam evaporation, laser patterning, glovebox processing, encapsulation, and cleanroom fabrication. I combine this with electrical, optical, structural, and morphological characterisation to understand how substrate roughness, interfaces, and processing conditions affect device performance.

Before beginning my PhD, I worked on wide-bandgap perovskite device modelling, multijunction photovoltaics, silicon disorder, SnO₂/rGO nanocomposites, and physical vapour deposition techniques for thin-film solar cells.

Research focus

Current doctoral research

01

Scalable spray-coated perovskite solar cells

Fabricating and optimising perovskite solar cells using scalable spray-coating approaches for active and functional layers, supported by spin coating, glovebox processing, evaporation, patterning, and encapsulation.

02

CFRP integration

Integrating thin-film photovoltaic devices with carbon-fibre-reinforced polymer substrates for lightweight and high-specific-power applications.

03

Interface and substrate engineering

Investigating substrate roughness, interfacial engineering, and processing conditions to improve morphology, crystallisation, and device performance.

04

Mini-module scale-up

Developing fabrication strategies to scale laboratory devices into series-connected mini-modules with active areas of 2.38 cm² and 11.62 cm².

05

Device characterisation

Relating electrical performance to optical, structural, and morphological properties through J–V, EQE, UV–Vis, PL, XRD, Raman, SEM, AFM, and profilometry.
